Tap your chest and particles flow out to cover your whole body. Could we really build an Iron Man nanosuit?
In the movies it starts with the 2018 Mark 50. It repairs itself and even makes thrusters that reach space.
In reality? In 2014, 1,024 robots at Harvard gathered themselves into a star.
But one shape took about 12 hours. Very different from the movie's one second.
In 2023, a metal robot melted, slipped through bars, and took its shape back.
There's even plastic that heals its own cracks, recovering up to about 75 percent of its strength.
The U.S. military tried to build an Iron Man suit but stopped in 2019. Weight and power were the problems.
A real flying jet suit has five engines, but it can only fly for about three to four minutes at a time.
By weight, jet fuel holds about 50 times more energy than batteries. The real challenge is energy.
And even if the suit holds up, a sudden takeoff is a problem for the brain of the person inside.
Bottom line: the whole suit is still far away, but its pieces are already growing in labs.
